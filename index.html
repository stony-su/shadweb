Short story

By Wes Wilson

It is bright inside the room. That’s the only thing I notice.
Stay awake, I tell myself. Just keep your eyes open for a little longer.
It won’t work for long. I know this; I’ve done it before. But it’s not like I have any better options.
How much longer is this class? I look at my wrist to check the time – and then remember that I took it off earlier.
So. No time, no space, no real sense of place.
Time is a construct, anyway. The class will end when it ends.
And I… will decidedly not stay awake until then. I fight to keep my eyelids open as my mind brushes against the edges of sleep.
I want to stay alert; everything the presenter is saying seems genuinely interesting, even if I’m not really hearing them. But despite that, and despite the fluorescent lights glaring overhead, I cannot help but fall away from the world of the waking. I let my eyes close, just for a second, and everything is gone.
Nothing really happens. My brain is not smoke and mist for long enough for me to wander any dreams, or even dredge up any thoughts. But that nothing is an extremely welcome break from the nonstop buzz of reality.
So I drift.
…
And then I am falling, and then I am awake. I jerk back up into a sitting position, hoping that nobody noticed the obvious dip of my head.
No one seems to react, and the presenter continues on.
This time, I’ve gotten away with it. I know I won’t be so lucky if I slip up again.
The weight of wakefulness is heavier than ever. I want nothing more than to step back into that blissful unconsciousness, but I need to do it carefully so as to not be caught.
Somehow, with my sleep-deprived brain, I need to make a smart decision.
The idea comes to me almost immediately, which cannot be a good sign. It’s very stupid.
…But there’s a non-zero chance it could work. And it’s not like I have any better options.
So I reach into my bag, pull out my sunglasses, and put them on.
The lenses are so reflective they’re basically opaque. It’s more or less impossible to tell if my eyes are open behind them (I hope). As far as my fraying rationalizations are concerned, it’s a perfect plan.
I close my eyes.
And everything is gone once again.

Rec By Shads: Houses 1 and 2

Daniel & Ruibo

As the first Rec By Shads drew near, so did a question in the hearts of us all. “Will this be able to stack up to the other Recs?”. It was a high bar to meet, but the creativity and innovations of Houses 1 and 2 did not fall short. House 1 collaboratively presented us with an engaging, unique game that tested our delegation abilities. The game encouraged us to adapt to everyone's unique skillset so that our house could get to the sandcastle making process the fastest. The creation of the sandcastles was frantic, but they were very effective at pushing our imaginative skills to the next level. Starting with a few student group battles, setting the stage for the showdown between Joe and Jon. The match ended up with Joe’s victory, completing his throws while Jon remained struggling. House 2’s game was more simple fun, being an anarchical game of handball that encouraged collaboration and active moving. Five groups fight on their own, everyone is locked in, moving fast. Darren summarizes the whole energetic session with a single word: “Chaotic.”
